Executive Summary

This research internship focused on mastering and deploying advanced Monte Carlo simulation frameworks—Geant4 and CORSIKA 7.8010—to model particle transport through matter and simulate particle interactions with the atmosphere. The core work combined detector geometry configuration, high-energy particle physics, Linux system management, and custom Python data engineering.

Geant4 Particle Tracking & Volume Interaction

I configured a Windows compilation environment utilizing Visual Studio Build Tools, CMake, and Geant4 batch scripts. During the internship, I executed simulations shooting 6 MeV gamma rays through custom trapezoidal and conical shapes, visualizing trajectories with interactive OpenGL scenes. I successfully modified the C++ simulation code to log precise step-by-step physical parameters, including 3D spatial coordinates, energy deposition, kinetic energy, and momentum.

CORSIKA Atmospheric Particle Simulation

Working in a Linux environment, I configured CORSIKA input files to define physical variables of particle interactions with the atmosphere, including primary particle types, specific energy ranges, incidence angles, and atmospheric profiles. I executed multi-particle runs to generate comprehensive statistical models of cascade physics and longitudinal/lateral development.

Python Data Engineering Pipeline

Because CORSIKA outputs in a highly optimized proprietary binary format, I developed custom Jupyter Notebook scripts utilizing Python’s struct library to decode the raw binary layout. Using NumPy and Pandas, I reshaped and structured the particle data, successfully converting complex "long files" into high-efficiency CSV files to analyze particle distribution counts and energy deposition mechanics across varying atmospheric depths.
